1. Meaning and Importance of Economics (pp. 2-3)

1.1 Meaning of Economics

Economics is a subject that studies the allocation of limited resources to satisfy the unlimited demand of mankind in the most beneficial way.

Starter trivia: Plato, Aristotle, and Adam Smith all studied economics long before it became a modern subject.

ThinkerContribution
PlatoFocus on the division of labor, the importance of moderation.
AristotleManagement of a household, focusing on production, consumption, and the distribution.
Adam SmithFree markets are the most productive and beneficial to their societies.

1.2 Importance of Economics — 3 Levels

Economics has different levels of importance:

  1. Personal and household level — in daily life, Economics helps people and households allocate their limited resources to be most beneficial (e.g. a monthly budget for rent, food, and savings).
  2. Producer level — Economics helps producers make decisions about what kind of goods to produce.
  3. Country level — the country or the government has to use economic knowledge to help manage the resources for its people.

2. Fundamental Economic Problems & Aims of Economics (pp. 4-5)

Fundamental economic problems are the problems that every country in the world has to face. They can be divided into three issues:

QuestionWhat it considers
1. What to produce?What products or services should be produced (goods vs. services).
2. How to produce?Which technology should be used in production.
3. For whom to produce?Whom the products should be allocated for, sold, or distributed.

Aims of Economics

The aim of Economics is to set guidelines or policies to solve the 3 economic problems of the limited resources management above — in other words, the fundamental problems define what needs solving, and the aims of Economics define why policy exists: to actually solve them.

3. Frameworks of Economics: Microeconomics vs. Macroeconomics (p. 6)

Economists separate the study of Economics into two main branches.

3.1 Microeconomics

The study of economic behavior at an individual level or an economic unit (e.g. family, and firms or company) — such as a study of consumer behaviors and producer behaviors.

Units studied: Individuals, Family/Households, Firms/Company/Producers.

  • A family compares the prices of rice before deciding which brand to buy.
  • A student saves money to buy a new laptop instead of spending it on snacks.
  • S&P lowers the price of its bread to attract more customers.

3.2 Macroeconomics

The study of the overall economic behavior of the country/the government — such as national income, money supply, consumption, saving, and investment.

Scope: Country and National Production.

  • When inflation rises, the prices of goods and services increase across the country.
  • The government builds new roads to create more jobs and improve the economy.

3.3 Quick Classification Reference

StatementBranch
High unemployment can slow down a nation's economic growth.Macroeconomics
When the price of gasoline increases, some people choose to use public transportation.Microeconomics

Rule of thumb: if the example is about ONE person, family, or firm → Microeconomics. If it's about the WHOLE country/government → Macroeconomics.

4. Resources in Economics — 4 Factors of Production (p. 8)

Vocabulary:

  • Resources — something that can be used for some benefit or to make a profit.
  • Producer — someone who creates and supplies goods or services.
  • Consumer — someone who buys goods or services for their own use.
  • Scarcity — a state of being in shortage of important resources.
FactorDefinition
LandUsed in cultivation or business, such as the location of a factory — includes resources under and on the surface of such land.
LaborThe human resources that use their own physical power and thought to produce goods and services.
CapitalA man-made element used for producing goods and services, such as machines and production equipment.
EntrepreneurThe unit which gathers all production factors. Entrepreneurs can be an organization, company, shop, or individual.

A real business needs all four working together: Land gives the location, Labor gives the workforce, Capital gives the equipment, and the Entrepreneur organizes all of them into a functioning operation.

5. Scarcity, Choice, and Opportunity Cost (pp. 9-10)

  • Scarcity is the result of imbalanced allocation of limited resources.
  • Choice is important to select suitable resources and to use them at their highest efficiency.
  • Opportunity cost is the highest value of the benefit that we may get from the choice that we did not choose. It is also called "Alternative cost."

"You want them both but you can only have one" — e.g. hamburger vs. chicken burger, KFC vs. McDonald's, mansion vs. sports car. Whichever you don't choose is your opportunity cost.

How the three ideas connect

Scarcity (limited resources) forces a Choice to be made among alternatives, and that Choice creates an Opportunity Cost — the value of the alternative given up. This same logic applies to the 3 fundamental economic problems in Section 2: deciding what, how, and for whom to produce all involve giving up some other use of the same limited resources.

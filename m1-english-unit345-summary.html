Grammar: Past simple of can

Affirmative: could. Negative: couldn't. Example: "Could you become real astronauts?" "Yes, they could. / No, they couldn't."

Grammar: Past simple — affirmative, negative, questions

  • Add -ed to make the past simple form of regular verbs (e.g. walk → walked).
  • Negative sentences use didn't before the infinitive form of the verb (e.g. didn't travel).
  • Questions use did + subject + the infinitive form of the verb (e.g. Did you go...?).
  • In short answers, we repeat the main verb: Yes, I did. / No, I didn't.

Spelling rules for regular past simple

RuleExample
Add -ed to most verbswalk → walked, need → needed
Verbs ending in -e: add -dlove → loved, decide → decided
Verbs ending in consonant + y: change y to iedtry → tried, study → studied
Verbs ending in one vowel + one consonant: double the consonant, add -edstop → stopped, shop → shopped

Reading highlights

  • Space Tourists: Dennis Tito (American, 2001) was the first space tourist. Others include Mark Shuttleworth (South African, 2002), Gregory Olsen (American, 2005), Anousheh Ansari (Iranian, first female, 2006), Charles Simonyi (Hungarian, 2007 & 2009), and Richard Garriott (American, 2008).
  • Richard Garriott's ticket cost $30 million; he spent 12 days on the ISS with NASA astronaut Michael Fincke and Russian cosmonaut Yuri Lonchakov. His childhood dream of being an astronaut was blocked by bad eyesight, so he made his fortune in video games instead.
  • Thomas Cook organised the first package holiday in 1841 (a 12-mile train excursion). By 1855 he ran the first international trip around Europe; by 1872, a 222-day round-the-world tour cost £210 (over £25,000 in today's money).
  • New Zealand: around 4 million tourists visit each year; the Nevis bungee jump is 134 metres; the Wildwire Wanaka cable climb is 450 metres; the world's smallest penguin (found at Pukekura) is only 25 cm tall; whale-watching tours in the Bay of Plenty take about 5 hours.

Reading: A story of two novelists

Suzanne Collins

Author of The Hunger Games. Born in Connecticut, US; her father was a soldier, so the family moved often. She first worked as a writer for children's TV, including the programme Generation O!, where fellow author James Proimos encouraged her to try writing novels for children. Her first series was The Underland Chronicles, but she became world-famous for The Hunger Games — she got the idea one night while switching between the news and a reality TV show and noticing how little difference there was between them.

Eoin Colfer

Author of books for children and young people, including the Artemis Fowl series. Born in Wexford, Ireland; both his parents were teachers. His love of writing came from exciting stories about Vikings he learned about at school. After university he worked as a teacher, then taught English in several countries across Africa and Asia. His experience in Tunisia inspired his first book, Benny and Omar. He wrote the first Artemis Fowl book — about a twelve-year-old criminal genius — in 2001, inspired by thinking one of his own sons resembled a villain from the James Bond films. Other books include Iron Man: The Gauntlet and Illegal.
